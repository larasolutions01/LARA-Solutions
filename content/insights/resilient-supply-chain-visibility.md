Supply chain resilience is often discussed in terms of advanced forecasting and optimisation. Those tools are valuable — but they depend on something more basic: **knowing what is happening right now.**

## The visibility problem

In many businesses, the status of an order, a shipment or a stock level is spread across email threads, carrier portals, ERP screens and spreadsheets. Answering a simple question — *“Will this customer’s order arrive on time?”* — can take several people and several systems.

## Step 1: Define the critical questions

Start with the questions your team asks most often:

- What is in transit, and is any of it late?
- Which products are at risk of running out?
- Which suppliers are consistently late or variable?

Each question tells you which data you need and how fresh it must be.

## Step 2: Bring the data together

Connect your order, inventory and shipment data into a single model. This might mean integrating carrier updates, ERP exports and warehouse systems. Data quality issues will surface — that’s a feature, not a bug. Fixing them improves every decision downstream.

## Step 3: Manage by exception

A dashboard showing everything quickly becomes a dashboard nobody reads. Instead, design **alerts and exception lists**: shipments running late, stock below safety levels, suppliers outside agreed lead times. Your team’s attention goes where it’s needed.

## Step 4: Add intelligence where it pays

With reliable visibility in place, more advanced capabilities become practical:

- **Demand forecasting** to support purchasing decisions.
- **Inventory optimisation** to balance availability with working capital.
- **Predicted delays** based on historical carrier and route performance.

## The takeaway

Resilience isn’t a single system. It’s the ability to see problems early and respond well. Visibility is the foundation — and it’s often the fastest, most practical place to start.
