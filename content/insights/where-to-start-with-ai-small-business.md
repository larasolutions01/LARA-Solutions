Most business owners we speak to aren’t asking *whether* AI matters. They’re asking a more useful question: **where do we actually start?**

The answer is rarely “with the most impressive technology.” It’s with the most annoying, repetitive, well-understood problem in your business.

## 1. Start with work, not with tools

Before looking at any AI product, list the tasks your team repeats every week. Good candidates usually share three traits:

- **They happen often** — daily or weekly, not once a quarter.
- **They follow recognisable patterns** — the same questions, documents or decisions in slightly different forms.
- **Mistakes are visible and fixable** — a person can review the output.

Answering routine customer emails, extracting details from invoices, summarising calls into your CRM and preparing weekly reports all tend to fit.

## 2. Estimate the value honestly

For each candidate, write down roughly how many hours it takes per week and who does it. You don’t need precision — you need a ranking. The task that consumes the most skilled time is usually the best first target, because the people freed up can move to higher-value work.

## 3. Check your data and process readiness

AI amplifies whatever you give it. If a process is inconsistent, or the information it depends on lives in five different places, fix that first. Sometimes the most valuable outcome of an “AI project” is a cleaner process that would have been worth doing anyway.

## 4. Run a small, measured pilot

Pick one use case. Agree a baseline — time spent, response times, error rates — and a clear definition of success. Keep a human in the loop at the start and review results weekly. A pilot that runs for a few weeks and teaches you something is far more valuable than a grand plan that never ships.

## 5. Decide what to scale

If the pilot works, expand it carefully: more volume, more channels, or the next use case on your list. If it doesn’t, you’ve learned cheaply — and you probably understand your process better than before.

## Common pitfalls to avoid

- **Automating a broken process.** You’ll just get broken results faster.
- **Skipping governance.** Decide early what data the system can access and when a human must review.
- **Measuring nothing.** Without a baseline, you can’t tell whether it worked.

## The bottom line

AI adoption for a small business isn’t a moonshot. It’s a series of practical, measurable improvements — each one freeing up time and building confidence for the next.
