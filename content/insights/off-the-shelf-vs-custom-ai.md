There has never been more AI software available off the shelf. Most business tools now include AI features, and new products launch weekly. So why would a small or medium-sized business pay for anything custom?

Sometimes it shouldn’t. Here is how we help clients decide.

## When off-the-shelf is the right answer

Choose an existing product when most of these are true:

- **The need is common.** Meeting transcription, writing assistance, email drafting, basic support chat and document search are well served by mature products.
- **The tool fits how you work.** You can use it as designed without spreadsheets or manual steps around it.
- **Your data can stay where it is** — or the tool connects natively to the systems you already use.
- **Speed matters more than differentiation.** You need value this month, and the process isn’t a source of competitive advantage.

Off-the-shelf tools are quick to trial, improve without effort on your part and usually cost a predictable subscription. Before committing, check how the vendor handles your data, whether you can export it, and what happens to your workflow if prices or features change.

## When custom automation pays off

A custom workflow — built around your process and connected to your systems — is worth considering when:

- **The process is specific to your business.** Your quoting rules, approval steps or product data don’t match any product’s assumptions.
- **The work spans several systems.** The value comes from moving information between your CRM, inbox, accounting and operations tools — something no single product does for you.
- **A tool would force a worse process.** If adopting a product means re-keying data or adding manual checks, you may be paying for the subscription *and* the workaround.
- **Control matters.** You need specific review steps, audit logs, data residency or behaviour that a general product doesn’t offer.

Custom does not have to mean large. Many effective automations are small, focused workflows that connect existing tools and use an AI model for one step — such as reading an incoming document or classifying a request.

## Compare the total cost, not the starting price

| Cost to consider | Off-the-shelf | Custom |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront | Low — setup and training | Higher — discovery, build and testing |
| Ongoing | Per-user or usage subscription | Hosting, model usage, monitoring |
| Integration | Often limited to built-in connectors | Designed for your systems |
| Change | Depends on the vendor’s roadmap | You decide, but you maintain it |
| Exit | Depends on data export options | You own the workflow and data flows |

The cheapest option in month one is not always the cheapest in year two. Equally, a custom build that needs constant attention can cost more than it saves. Estimate both over a realistic period, including the time your team spends working around limitations.

## The hybrid approach most businesses end up with

In practice, the answer is rarely all one or the other. A common pattern is:

1. Use off-the-shelf products for common, standalone needs.
2. Connect them with a small amount of [custom integration](/solutions/technology-integration) so data flows without re-keying.
3. Build custom AI steps only where the process is distinctive and the value is clear.

## Questions to ask before you commit

- What exactly will this tool or workflow do, step by step, in our process?
- How will we measure whether it works — and what is our baseline today?
- Where does our data go, who can access it, and can we get it back out?
- What happens when it gets something wrong? Who reviews, and how?
- Who maintains it after launch?
